The short answer

The 15% shift is not supported by the data. It is not disproven either.

The fix works

Splitting each TV week across its days stays within noise of perfect data: P(shift helps) moves by +0.01 on average, never a confident wrong call.

One shortcut adds false confidence

Giving each whole week to the month it starts in raises P(shift helps) above perfect data in 22 of 25 runs, and 4 times called a harmful shift “Holds”.

The model leans too

Even on perfect data, a monthly model recovers about half of TV’s true marginal ROI, and default priors put email at 11% of sales (truth: 4%).

Rule fixed before any fit: the shift holds if P(it raises sales) ≥ 0.80
Headline: not supported, not disproven. Three findings support it. The calendar fix recovers what perfect data would say. The week-start shortcut systematically inflates confidence in the shift. And independent of calendars, equal priors on scaled spend inflate the smallest channel, email.
