Finding 2: the model, not the calendar

Default priors inflate the smallest channel, even on perfect data

EMAIL SHARE OF SALES, EQUAL PRIOR

11.1%

Same prior for every channel on spend scaled by its own average: a dollar of email starts out worth several dollars of TV.

SPEND-SHARE PRIOR

5.0%

Prior effect in proportion to each channel’s share of spend.

TRUTH

4.2%

What the simulated world actually gave email.

Exhibit 2 flagged email at 11% as “high efficiency”. That number deserves a second look on the real data. With equal priors, perfect data said “Holds” in 6 of 13 runs, once wrongly; with spend-share priors, in none.

Perfect data here means the oracle version, so this bias has nothing to do with the calendar. The 11% match with Exhibit 2 is a coincidence of our setup, but it shows how a contribution share like that can come from the prior. Spend-share priors are the approach PyMC-Marketing's docs recommend; they can understate a genuinely efficient small channel, so the real fix is calibrating with lift tests.
