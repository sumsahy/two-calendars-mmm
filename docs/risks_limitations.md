# Risks and limitations: what this work does NOT prove

| # | Limitation | Why it matters | What would fix it |
|---|---|---|---|
| L-01 | **Synthetic data proves the mechanism, not the client's answer** | Our verdicts describe simulated worlds. The client's real recommendation can only be judged by rerunning on their real data | Run `corrected` vs the original rollup on real data (same code path) |
| L-02 | **36 monthly data points is thin** | Intervals are wide; even perfect data (oracle) rarely clears the 0.80 bar. The original recommendation may never have been strongly supported | Weekly sales (IQVIA weekly TRx exists in pharma), geo-level data, or a geo holdout test |
| L-03 | **Few random seeds, and the 7 scenarios share one seed** | 4 histories for the two decisive worlds, 1 for the rest. Scenarios differ in true parameters but reuse the same random draws (e.g. identical TV flighting), so they are not independent worlds. Monte Carlo SE of each P is about 0.02 | Give each scenario its own seeds; more seeds overnight |
| L-04 | **Spec-reader score is not independent** | Claude wrote both the eval labels and the recorded readings | Sumukh reviews labels; Groq live run (`make llm-live`) gives an independent reader |
| L-05 | **The monthly model cannot represent the true response (R-01)** | Truth saturates day by day; the model saturates monthly totals. On perfect data it recovers a median of about half of TV's true marginal ROI, which tilts every version toward "the shift helps" | Apply adstock and saturation at daily grain, then sum to months (option A in D-019, not run) |
| L-06 | **Priors drive the answer** (D-015, R-02) | Equal priors on scaled spend inflate email's share; the saturation prior (Gamma(2,1)) pulls every mROI down (email 1.12 vs truth 1.90 under spend-share) | Looser saturation prior; calibrate with lift tests, as Meridian and Robyn support |
| L-07 | **Even-delivery assumption in the fix** | The fix spreads each week evenly over 7 days; real delivery is weekend-heavy | Measured residual error about 1-2% of an average month; daily delivery logs would remove it |
| L-08 | **Monthly adstock is coarse, and month 1 has no carryover** | TV with a 14-day half-life mostly decays within the month; the first month's adstock is zero-padded while the truth has 120 days of history | Transform at daily grain before aggregating; feed pre-window spend or drop the first months from the likelihood |
| L-09 | **Not every fit converged** | One fit had r-hat 1.52 and 234 divergences; several have r-hat 1.01-1.05. Fits over r-hat 1.05 or 20 divergences are flagged and excluded from tallies (D-020). Equal-prior sweep used 1,000 draws, spend-share 600 | 4+ chains, higher target_accept, same sampler settings everywhere |
| L-10 | **Revenue, not Rx** | Pharma MMMs often model TRx/NRx | Same code; swap the target column |
| L-11 | **The main-analysis prior was chosen after seeing results** | Only the 0.80 threshold and the scenarios were committed before fitting. The spend-share prior came after the equal-prior sweep (raised openly as D-015, but post hoc) | Pre-register prior choice next time; we report both priors side by side |
| L-12 | **The gate cannot catch a units change** (e.g. spend sent in $000) | Needs a baseline from the previous accepted refresh | Add a drift check against the last accepted drop |
| L-13 | **Some priors sit close to the known truth** | Intercept prior centred at 0.7 (true baseline share about 0.7); noise prior near the true 3% | Wider, truth-blind priors |
